Normal maps and channel packing: doing ORM, roughness and metallic right

From height maps to normal maps, ORM / RMA channel packing and Y-axis conventions — the color-space and channel-order traps worth avoiding.

Normal maps2026-09-23·3 min read

Key takeaways

  • Treat normal maps as linear data
  • Unity and Unreal use different channel conventions
  • A wrong Y axis flips lighting globally

A normal map looks like “a bluish image”, but it stores vectors, not color. Treating it like an ordinary texture usually produces odd lighting, washed-out edges and banding after compression.

Normal maps are data, not color

RGB encodes the X, Y and Z directions mapped into 0 to 1. It must be handled as linear data: no sRGB conversion, no photo-oriented compression settings, no casual color grading.

When generating from a height map, the strength parameter controls exaggeration. Too strong and details intersect; too weak and the surface disappears under large lights. Tune it under real engine lighting rather than a flat preview.

Y axis: OpenGL versus DirectX

Graphics APIs disagree about the green channel. OpenGL conventionally points Y up; DirectX points Y down. Getting it backwards flips lighting globally — bumps read as dents.

Unity and Unreal default differently, so cross-engine reuse requires checking this. TexForge exposes a Y-flip switch when generating normal maps, so you do not have to invert the green channel by hand afterwards.

Channel packing: ORM and RMA

To reduce texture count, artists pack ambient occlusion, roughness and metallic into three channels. Two conventions dominate:

  • ORM: R = AO, G = Roughness, B = Metallic — common in Unreal workflows.
  • RMA: R = Roughness, G = Metallic, B = AO — used by some teams and pipelines.
  • Unity HDRP / URP have their own occlusion and smoothness habits, where smoothness is often the inverse of roughness.

One channel out of order and the material is visibly wrong: metal areas turn rough, cavity occlusion lands on highlights. Batch packing helps because the mapping is configured once and applied identically to every map.

Common traps

  1. Saving a normal map as sRGB so the engine applies gamma twice.
  2. Storing normal maps as JPEG, where compression noise becomes wrong normals.
  3. Packing a grayscale placeholder instead of actually baking AO.
  4. Mixing roughness and smoothness without the 1-minus inversion.
  5. Using one channel order across platforms that expect different ones.

Encode it in the batch

Channel mapping, Y direction and color-space flags are parameters you should decide once. Put them in a preset and process them alongside size and format — that is what makes results stable.

Takeaway

The hard part of normal maps and channel packing is convention, not tooling. Confirm the engine, the color space and the channel order, then let batch processing guarantee consistency.
